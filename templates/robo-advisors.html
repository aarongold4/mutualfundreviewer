Robo-Advisors & Brokers: The Honest Trade-Off

A robo-advisor costs more than buying an index fund yourself. We’ll say that first, plainly — and then explain why it can still be the right choice for some investors.

The math is simple. Holding VTSAX or FZROX yourself costs you near zero. A typical robo-advisor adds about 0.25% a year on top of the funds it buys. On a large balance over decades, that gap is real money — run it through the calculator and you’ll see.

So why would anyone pay it? Because the most expensive mistake in investing isn’t a slightly higher fee — it’s panic-selling in a downturn, never rebalancing, or never starting at all. If automation is what keeps you invested and on course, 0.25% is a bargain for the behavior it buys. The honest framing: a robo-advisor is cheap insurance against yourself, and pure overhead if you didn’t need the insurance. Only you know which describes you.

The DIY alternative, in one paragraph

If you decide the automation isn’t worth the fee, the do-it-yourself version is genuinely simple: open a free brokerage account, buy a single target-date index fund (or a total-market fund plus a bond fund), set up an automatic monthly contribution, and turn off the app. That’s the entire robo-advisor service — diversification, rebalancing, discipline — minus the 0.25%, for anyone willing to do roughly an hour of setup and then nothing. Both paths are respectable. The unforgivable one is doing neither.
