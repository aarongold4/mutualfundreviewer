The Best Index Funds, Ranked by Cost

For each category that matters in a long-term portfolio, here are the index funds worth owning — ranked the only way that reliably predicts results: by expense ratio, lowest first.

You will notice the picks within each category are nearly interchangeable. That is the point. The index-fund price war has been so thoroughly won by investors that choosing between the top funds in any category is mostly a question of which brokerage holds your account. We’ve flagged the meaningful tie-breakers — minimums, portability, index quirks — but if you walked away owning any fund in the top two of each list below, you’d have done the hard part right.

Building a portfolio from these

You don’t need one fund from every table. The classic, evidence-backed portfolios use two to four:

The one-fund portfolio: a single target-date fund matched to your retirement year, or a total-world stock fund plus a bond fund. Done.

The three-fund portfolio: a total US market fund, a total international fund, and a total bond fund — you set the percentages, rebalance once a year, and never touch a sector or style table above.

The style boxes — growth, value, small-cap, REITs — are refinements for people who want to tilt deliberately, not requirements. A beginner who owns nothing but a target-date fund from the last table is better diversified than most professionals.
