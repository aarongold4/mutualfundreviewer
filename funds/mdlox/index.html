Go-anywhere global multi-asset investing — equities, bonds, cash, and currencies across all markets.

Global Allocation was the advisor channel's 'one fund to own' of the 1990s and 2000s: a globe-spanning team shifting among stocks, bonds, and currencies, with a strong record under founding manager Dennis Stattman. The pitch — let professionals make the asset-allocation calls — built it into one of the largest multi-asset funds ever.

The pitch has aged worse than the fund. Post-Stattman performance has been ordinary, and the structural problem is the math: to beat a static, self-rebalancing 60/40 index blend costing 0.07% at a 1.10% fee (plus a possible 5.25% load), the team must win its macro calls persistently — the hardest game in investing. Two decades of evidence on tactical funds says the calls mostly cancel out and the fee remains. BlackRock's own iShares division, which sells the cheap building blocks, is the quiet counterargument from inside the building.

Best for

Investors drawn to a global tactical allocator — eyes open about the fee era it comes from.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of MDLOX?

MDLOX (BlackRock Global Allocation Fund (Class A)) has an expense ratio of 1.10%, which works out to roughly $110 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is high relative to index alternatives, and it compounds against you every year, so the fund needs to outperform just to break even versus a cheaper option.

What is the minimum investment for MDLOX?

MDLOX requires a minimum initial investment of $1,000.

What does MDLOX invest in?

Go-anywhere global multi-asset investing — equities, bonds, cash, and currencies across all markets. It falls in the balanced / allocation category and is an actively managed fund.

What are good alternatives to MDLOX?

The funds most often compared with MDLOX are VBIAX, VTWAX, FPACX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
