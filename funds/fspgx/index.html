Tracks the Russell 1000 Growth Index — large US companies with growth characteristics.

FSPGX exists as a standing rebuke to Fidelity's own famous growth funds: it tracks the Russell 1000 Growth — the benchmark Contrafund and Blue Chip Growth are measured against — for 0.035%, versus the 0.4–0.5% those celebrated active funds charge. Over the last decade-plus, the dumb benchmark has been brutally hard for the stars to beat after fees.

What you own here is concentration, dressed as diversification: growth indexes are mechanically dominated by a handful of mega-cap tech names, so this fund's fate is tied to theirs. That's been a wonderful place to be tied recently and a catastrophic one in 2000–2002. If your total-market fund's 30%+ tech weight already feels like enough growth exposure — it probably is — this fund is an amplifier you don't need.

Best for

Growth-style exposure without growth-manager fees.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of FSPGX?

FSPGX (Fidelity Large Cap Growth Index Fund) has an expense ratio of 0.035%, which works out to roughly $3.50 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is very low by industry standards, where the average fund still charges well over 0.40%.

What is the minimum investment for FSPGX?

FSPGX has no minimum initial investment — you can start with any amount.

What does FSPGX invest in?

Tracks the Russell 1000 Growth Index — large US companies with growth characteristics. It falls in the large growth category and is an index fund.

What are good alternatives to FSPGX?

The funds most often compared with FSPGX are VIGAX, FBGRX, TRBCX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
