Indexes a fixed 60% US total stock market / 40% US total bond market allocation.

The 60/40 portfolio gets pronounced dead every few years — most loudly in 2022, when both sides fell together — and then goes back to quietly doing its job. VBIAX is the purest implementation available: 60% total US stock index, 40% total US bond index, automatically rebalanced, 0.07%, no glide path, no manager opinions, no international wrinkle.

It's an underrated answer for money that needs to be sensible rather than optimized — an inheritance you don't want to actively manage, a conservative IRA, the equity-lite years before retirement. The main critique is the absence of international stocks, which makes it a deliberate home-country bet. Compare it with Wellington (VWELX) if you're curious what an active manager adds to roughly this allocation for an extra 19 basis points; the answer over most periods has been 'not much, reliably.'

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of VBIAX?

VBIAX (Vanguard Balanced Index Fund Admiral Shares) has an expense ratio of 0.07%, which works out to roughly $7 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is very low by industry standards, where the average fund still charges well over 0.40%.

What is the minimum investment for VBIAX?

VBIAX requires a minimum initial investment of $3,000.

What does VBIAX invest in?

Indexes a fixed 60% US total stock market / 40% US total bond market allocation. It falls in the balanced / allocation category and is an index fund.

What are good alternatives to VBIAX?

The funds most often compared with VBIAX are VWELX, FBALX, VBTLX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
