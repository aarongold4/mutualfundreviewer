Actively managed target-date fund of T. Rowe funds, de-risking toward 2050; currently around 90% equities.

Millions of workers own TRRMX because their employer's plan is administered by T. Rowe Price and this is the default — which makes it worth saying plainly: as defaults go, this is a good one. Diversified, equity-rich for the horizon, professionally rebalanced, and run by a firm whose active funds are better than average. A young saver auto-enrolled here is being treated decently.

Decently, but not cheaply. The ~0.61% expense ratio is the toll for active underlying funds, and over 25 years it compounds into real money versus an 0.08% index series. The practical advice: contribute happily to TRRMX while it's your plan's best option, capture every employer match — and when you change jobs, roll the balance to an IRA where the index target-date series cost one-seventh as much. The glide path travels; the fee doesn't have to.

Best for

Younger savers defaulted into T. Rowe-administered 401(k) plans.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of TRRMX?

TRRMX (T. Rowe Price Retirement 2050 Fund) has an expense ratio of 0.61%, which works out to roughly $61 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is high relative to index alternatives, and it compounds against you every year, so the fund needs to outperform just to break even versus a cheaper option.

What is the minimum investment for TRRMX?

TRRMX requires a minimum initial investment of $2,500.

What does TRRMX invest in?

Actively managed target-date fund of T. Rowe funds, de-risking toward 2050; currently around 90% equities. It falls in the target-date category and is a target-date fund.

What are good alternatives to TRRMX?

The funds most often compared with TRRMX are VFIFX, FIPFX, SWYMX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
