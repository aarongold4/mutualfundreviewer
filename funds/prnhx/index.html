Active small/mid-cap growth investing in emerging companies, including pre-IPO positions; closed to new investors.

New Horizons is one of the legendary small-cap growth funds — six decades old, an early backer of countless companies before they were famous, and under Henry Ellenbogen's 2010s tenure, the rare fund whose pre-IPO sleeve added real, measurable value. It closed to new investors years ago, which is both a disappointment and the responsible move: small-cap alpha dies of asset bloat faster than any other kind.

For the readers who hold it: the post-Ellenbogen era has been rockier, with the 2022 growth unwind hitting hard, and the fund's size forces it ever further up the cap spectrum from its small-cap roots. For everyone else, the lesson generalizes nicely — when an active fund is good enough to be worth buying, it's usually closed, and when it's open to you, ask why. Index funds never face this paradox; VSGAX takes all comers at 0.07% precisely because it isn't trying to be clever.

Best for

Existing holders only — closed, and a study in small-cap capacity limits.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of PRNHX?

PRNHX (T. Rowe Price New Horizons Fund) has an expense ratio of 0.80%, which works out to roughly $80 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is high relative to index alternatives, and it compounds against you every year, so the fund needs to outperform just to break even versus a cheaper option.

What is the minimum investment for PRNHX?

PRNHX requires a minimum initial investment of $2,500.

What does PRNHX invest in?

Active small/mid-cap growth investing in emerging companies, including pre-IPO positions; closed to new investors. It falls in the small cap category and is an actively managed fund.

What are good alternatives to PRNHX?

The funds most often compared with PRNHX are VSGAX, VSMAX, FSSNX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
