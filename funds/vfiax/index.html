Tracks the S&P 500 — the 500 largest US companies, about 80% of total US market value.

This is the direct descendant of the first index fund ever sold to the public — Jack Bogle's 1976 'folly' that now anchors trillions of dollars in retirement money. VFIAX holds the S&P 500 in its market weights and charges 0.04% to do it. Over the decades, that simple recipe has beaten the large majority of professionally managed large-cap funds, which is either embarrassing for the profession or liberating for you, depending on where you sit.

Choosing between VFIAX and a total-market fund like VTSAX is a low-stakes decision dressed up as a big one. The S&P 500 is about four-fifths of the US market by value, and the two funds' long-run returns track each other closely. VTSAX adds small and mid caps; VFIAX is the purer blue-chip bet. Pick either, fund it relentlessly, and stop reading comparison articles — including, eventually, this one.

One practical note: inside many 401(k) plans, the S&P 500 index option is the cheapest fund on the menu and sometimes the only index option at all. If that's your situation, an S&P 500 fund at institutional pricing is an entirely sufficient core holding. The missing 20% of the market is a refinement, not a requirement.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of VFIAX?

VFIAX (Vanguard 500 Index Fund Admiral Shares) has an expense ratio of 0.04%, which works out to roughly $4 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is very low by industry standards, where the average fund still charges well over 0.40%.

What is the minimum investment for VFIAX?

VFIAX requires a minimum initial investment of $3,000.

What does VFIAX invest in?

Tracks the S&P 500 — the 500 largest US companies, about 80% of total US market value. It falls in the s&p 500 / large blend category and is an index fund.

What are good alternatives to VFIAX?

The funds most often compared with VFIAX are FXAIX, SWPPX, VTSAX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
