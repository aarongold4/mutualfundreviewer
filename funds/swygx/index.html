All-index target-date fund de-risking toward 2040, built from Schwab index funds and ETFs.

Schwab's Target Index series is the least famous of the big three all-index target-date lineups and arguably the friendliest: 0.08% — matching Vanguard, undercutting Fidelity's Freedom Index — with a $1 minimum that makes the 'I don't have enough to start' excuse extinct. The 2040 fund currently runs equity-heavy and walks down the standard glide path.

As with all of these series, the construction differences (Schwab uses some of its own ETFs inside, glide paths differ by a few percentage points at various ages) are real but minor, and the deciding factor is where your accounts live. The headline is that a saver at any of the three big retail brokerages can now own a complete, self-managing, all-index retirement portfolio for eight to twelve basis points. That sentence took fifty years of fee wars to become true.

Best for

One-fund retirement investing at Schwab with a $1 minimum.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of SWYGX?

SWYGX (Schwab Target 2040 Index Fund) has an expense ratio of 0.08%, which works out to roughly $8 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is very low by industry standards, where the average fund still charges well over 0.40%.

What is the minimum investment for SWYGX?

SWYGX requires a minimum initial investment of $1.

What does SWYGX invest in?

All-index target-date fund de-risking toward 2040, built from Schwab index funds and ETFs. It falls in the target-date category and is a target-date fund.

What are good alternatives to SWYGX?

The funds most often compared with SWYGX are VFORX, FBIFX, SWYMX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
