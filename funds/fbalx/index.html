Actively managed ~60% stocks / 40% bonds, with the equity sleeve run growth-tilted by sector specialists.

FBALX is the 60/40 with Fidelity's personality: the stock side leans growthier and techier than balanced-fund tradition, which has powered it past most peers — and past Vanguard's stately Wellington — across the growth-led 2010s and 2020s. Credit where due: among active balanced funds, its long-term record is near the top of the class.

The two honest asterisks: the growth tilt giveth and taketh away (2022 was unkind), and 0.44% is roughly six times VBIAX's fee for the same basic job. The index-fund argument writes itself. The counterargument is that FBALX has, so far, out-earned its fee — the rarest claim in this industry — and a one-fund investor who'll actually hold it through a bad growth year could do far worse.

Best for

A one-fund 60/40 with a growth lean and a solid record.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of FBALX?

FBALX (Fidelity Balanced Fund) has an expense ratio of 0.44%, which works out to roughly $44 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is moderate — cheap for an actively managed fund, but several times what a comparable index fund charges.

What is the minimum investment for FBALX?

FBALX has no minimum initial investment — you can start with any amount.

What does FBALX invest in?

Actively managed ~60% stocks / 40% bonds, with the equity sleeve run growth-tilted by sector specialists. It falls in the balanced / allocation category and is an actively managed fund.

What are good alternatives to FBALX?

The funds most often compared with FBALX are VBIAX, FPURX, VWELX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
