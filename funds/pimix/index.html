Multi-sector active bond investing optimized for high current income — mortgages, credit, global rates.

PIMCO Income under Dan Ivascyn became the post-Gross flagship for a reason: since 2007 it has generated standout income-oriented returns, largely through a savvy long-running position in non-agency mortgages bought cheap after the housing crash. Among multisector bond funds, it has been the category's reference point for over a decade.

Know what it is, though: a credit-risk fund in a bond fund's clothing. Its yield advantage over the Agg comes from owning things the Agg doesn't — and in true risk-off panics (March 2020 was the live demonstration) it falls with risk assets exactly when you want bonds standing still. As a yield-enhancing satellite next to a boring core like VBTLX, it earns its 0.51%. As your entire bond allocation, it quietly rewrites your portfolio's job description.

Best for

Income investors who understand they're holding a credit fund, not a Treasury fund.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of PIMIX?

PIMIX (PIMCO Income Fund (Institutional)) has an expense ratio of 0.51%, which works out to roughly $51 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is high relative to index alternatives, and it compounds against you every year, so the fund needs to outperform just to break even versus a cheaper option.

What is the minimum investment for PIMIX?

The institutional share class of PIMIX carries a $1,000,000 minimum, but most retail investors access it inside a 401(k) plan, where the minimum is waived. Retail share classes with lower minimums exist at higher fees.

What does PIMIX invest in?

Multi-sector active bond investing optimized for high current income — mortgages, credit, global rates. It falls in the bond category and is an actively managed fund.

What are good alternatives to PIMIX?

The funds most often compared with PIMIX are DODIX, PTTRX, VBTLX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
