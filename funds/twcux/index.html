Momentum-informed growth investing in companies with accelerating earnings — the house style since 1981.

Ultra is one of the oldest momentum-growth funds in America — American Century's founder Jim Stowers built the firm on 'earnings acceleration' before academics blessed momentum as a factor. The fund buys what's working and rides it, producing the return pattern the literature predicts: streaks of beating the market punctuated by sharp reversals when momentum crashes, as it periodically and violently does.

The modern problem is price: momentum is now a documented factor available in index and ETF form for a fraction of Ultra's 0.87%, and the fund's mega-cap-heavy portfolio overlaps heavily with any growth index. Its long-term record is respectable, its strategy honest, and its fee belongs to the decade when this approach was a secret. It no longer is.

Best for

Aggressive growth investors comfortable with momentum's whiplash.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of TWCUX?

TWCUX (American Century Ultra Fund (Investor)) has an expense ratio of 0.87%, which works out to roughly $87 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is high relative to index alternatives, and it compounds against you every year, so the fund needs to outperform just to break even versus a cheaper option.

What is the minimum investment for TWCUX?

TWCUX requires a minimum initial investment of $2,500.

What does TWCUX invest in?

Momentum-informed growth investing in companies with accelerating earnings — the house style since 1981. It falls in the large growth category and is an actively managed fund.

What are good alternatives to TWCUX?

The funds most often compared with TWCUX are FSPGX, VIGAX, FBGRX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
