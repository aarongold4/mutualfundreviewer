Subadvised by Jennison Associates: high-conviction investing in rapidly growing large companies.

Harbor's model is renting institutional managers to the public: HACAX is run by Jennison Associates, a growth shop institutions pay handsomely, packaged at a 0.67% retail-reachable fee (the $50,000 institutional-class minimum mostly dissolves inside retirement plans). Jennison's style is unapologetic high-growth conviction — the portfolio reads like a bet on the fastest-compounding franchises on earth.

The return pattern is textbook aggressive growth: spectacular in risk-on years, brutal in unwinds like 2022, with a long-term record that has genuinely contended with the Russell 1000 Growth more than most. Whether contending justifies 0.67% versus an index at 0.035% is the arithmetic that haunts every fund in this category; what HACAX offers over rivals is mainly pedigree and process consistency. A serious active growth fund — which is different from necessary.

Best for

Access to a respected institutional growth manager — mostly via retirement plans.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of HACAX?

HACAX (Harbor Capital Appreciation Fund (Institutional)) has an expense ratio of 0.67%, which works out to roughly $67 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is high relative to index alternatives, and it compounds against you every year, so the fund needs to outperform just to break even versus a cheaper option.

What is the minimum investment for HACAX?

HACAX requires a minimum initial investment of $50,000.

What does HACAX invest in?

Subadvised by Jennison Associates: high-conviction investing in rapidly growing large companies. It falls in the large growth category and is an actively managed fund.

What are good alternatives to HACAX?

The funds most often compared with HACAX are FSPGX, TRBCX, FBGRX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
