Actively managed ~60% stocks / 40% bonds; Fidelity's oldest balanced fund, slightly more value-aware than FBALX.

Puritan has been running the stocks-plus-bonds play since 1947 — through fourteen recessions, a dozen Fed regimes, and every market fad since nylon stockings. It's the marginally more conservative sibling to FBALX: similar 60/40 frame, a touch less growth-chasing in the equity sleeve, which typically means smaller swings in both directions.

The case for either Fidelity balanced fund over an index alternative rests entirely on management out-earning the ~0.40% fee gap, and Puritan's record makes that argument respectably if not overwhelmingly. The quieter point: funds like this exist mostly so an investor makes one decision instead of five. If that's the actual product — bundled discipline — judge it on whether you'd really rebalance a DIY 60/40 yourself in a crash. Many people honestly wouldn't.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of FPURX?

FPURX (Fidelity Puritan Fund) has an expense ratio of 0.47%, which works out to roughly $47 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is moderate — cheap for an actively managed fund, but several times what a comparable index fund charges.

What is the minimum investment for FPURX?

FPURX has no minimum initial investment — you can start with any amount.

What does FPURX invest in?

Actively managed ~60% stocks / 40% bonds; Fidelity's oldest balanced fund, slightly more value-aware than FBALX. It falls in the balanced / allocation category and is an actively managed fund.

What are good alternatives to FPURX?

The funds most often compared with FPURX are FBALX, VBIAX, VWELX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
