Glide-path fund of four Vanguard index funds, currently around 90% stocks / 10% bonds.

VFIFX is what 'set it and forget it' actually looks like: 90% global stock indexes, 10% bonds, rebalanced automatically, de-risking on autopilot starting in the 2030s, for less than a streaming subscription per $10,000 invested. It's the default fund in countless 401(k) plans, and unusually for defaults, it's close to what a fee-only advisor would build anyway.

The strongest argument for it over a DIY three-fund portfolio isn't the construction — you can replicate that — it's the firewall against yourself. Every rebalancing decision you never make is a market-timing mistake you never make. The strongest argument against is taxes: in a taxable account, the all-in-one structure forfeits foreign tax credits and asset-location tricks. In a 401(k) or IRA, it's about as close to optimal-by-default as the industry gets.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of VFIFX?

VFIFX (Vanguard Target Retirement 2050 Fund) has an expense ratio of 0.08%, which works out to roughly $8 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is very low by industry standards, where the average fund still charges well over 0.40%.

What is the minimum investment for VFIFX?

VFIFX requires a minimum initial investment of $1,000.

What does VFIFX invest in?

Glide-path fund of four Vanguard index funds, currently around 90% stocks / 10% bonds. It falls in the target-date category and is a target-date fund.

What are good alternatives to VFIFX?

The funds most often compared with VFIFX are SWYMX, FIPFX, VFFVX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
