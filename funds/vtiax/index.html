Tracks the FTSE Global All Cap ex US Index — about 8,500 stocks across developed and emerging markets outside the United States.

Whether to own international stocks at all is one of the few arguments that still divides otherwise-agreeing index investors. Bogle himself was famously lukewarm on the idea; Vanguard's own target-date funds put roughly 40% of equities abroad. If you land on the 'yes' side, VTIAX is the cleanest single answer: every developed and emerging market outside the US, large caps through small, in one fund at 0.09%.

The argument for holding it isn't that international will beat the US — nobody knows — but that you don't have to choose. US and international stocks have traded decade-long winning streaks for a century, and a 2010s-style stretch of US dominance is exactly the environment that tempts investors to abandon foreign stocks right before the pattern flips. VTIAX is how you opt out of that timing game.

It pairs naturally with VTSAX in the classic two-fund equity portfolio, and Vanguard's ETF share class (VXUS) is the identical portfolio if you prefer ETFs. Fidelity's FTIHX and the zero-fee FZILX are the closest competitors; FZILX is cheaper but skips international small caps and can't be transferred out of Fidelity in kind.

Best for

One-fund international diversification alongside a US core.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of VTIAX?

VTIAX (Vanguard Total International Stock Index Fund Admiral Shares) has an expense ratio of 0.09%, which works out to roughly $9 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is very low by industry standards, where the average fund still charges well over 0.40%.

What is the minimum investment for VTIAX?

VTIAX requires a minimum initial investment of $3,000.

What does VTIAX invest in?

Tracks the FTSE Global All Cap ex US Index — about 8,500 stocks across developed and emerging markets outside the United States. It falls in the international category and is an index fund.

What are good alternatives to VTIAX?

The funds most often compared with VTIAX are FTIHX, FZILX, SWISX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
