Tracks the Bloomberg US Aggregate Float Adjusted Index — investment-grade US government, corporate, and mortgage-backed bonds.

Bonds in a long-term portfolio are shock absorbers, not engines, and VBTLX is the standard-issue shock absorber: roughly 11,000 investment-grade US bonds, about two-thirds government-backed, with intermediate duration. It will not make you rich. Its job is to be the thing you sell to buy stocks when stocks are on sale, and to keep a 2008 or a 2022 from becoming the day you panic.

2022 deserves a frank mention: rising rates handed VBTLX its worst year on record, down about 13%, and a lot of investors discovered that 'safe' and 'can't lose money in a given year' are different claims. The silver lining is mechanical — higher rates mean higher yields going forward, and a bond index fund's long-run return converges on its starting yield. The fund is a better deal after a bad year, not a worse one.

If you want simpler still, the target-date and LifeStrategy funds hold this same portfolio internally. If you're in a high tax bracket holding bonds in a taxable account, a municipal bond fund may fit better — that's a tax question, not a fund-quality question.

Frequently asked questions

What is the expense ratio of VBTLX?

VBTLX (Vanguard Total Bond Market Index Fund Admiral Shares) has an expense ratio of 0.05%, which works out to roughly $5 per year for every $10,000 invested. That is very low by industry standards, where the average fund still charges well over 0.40%.

What is the minimum investment for VBTLX?

VBTLX requires a minimum initial investment of $3,000.

What does VBTLX invest in?

Tracks the Bloomberg US Aggregate Float Adjusted Index — investment-grade US government, corporate, and mortgage-backed bonds. It falls in the bond category and is an index fund.

What are good alternatives to VBTLX?

The funds most often compared with VBTLX are FXNAX, SWAGX, VTABX — broadly similar exposure from different providers, with small differences in fee, minimum, and portability that usually come down to which brokerage holds your account.
