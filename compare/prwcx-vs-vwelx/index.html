The bottom line

Both are rare active funds worth the fee. PRWCX has the more spectacular recent record but is mostly closed to new investors and rests on one manager; VWELX is open, institutionally durable, and cheaper at 0.26%. If you can't get PRWCX (most can't), VWELX is no consolation prize.

About PRWCX

PRWCX is the active fund that index-fund writers bring up when they want to seem fair, because the record is absurd: under David Giroux it has beaten its category in essentially every rolling multi-year period for nearly two decades, with a flexible stocks-bonds-convertibles mandate executed with unusual discipline. If skill exists in mutual fund management, the strongest evidence files live here.

About VWELX

Wellington launched four months before the 1929 crash and survived it, which remains the best opening line in mutual fund history. Nearly a century later it's still doing the same unfashionable thing: roughly two-thirds blue-chip dividend stocks, one-third high-grade bonds, picked by Wellington Management's value-leaning teams for 0.26% — expensive by index standards, absurdly cheap by active-management standards.
