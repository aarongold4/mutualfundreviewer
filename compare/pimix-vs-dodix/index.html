PIMIX vs. DODIX: PIMCO Income Fund (Institutional) or Dodge & Cox Income Fund?

Figures last verified October 31, 2026

Two of the most respected active bond funds, with very different risk dials. PIMCO Income reaches for yield through credit and mortgages; Dodge & Cox Income is a more measured core-plus fund.

The bottom line

PIMIX has the higher yield and the standout long-term record — but it's a credit-risk fund that falls in risk-off panics. DODIX is steadier and cheaper (0.41% vs 0.51%), closer to a true core holding. For a yield satellite, PIMIX; for the dependable bond anchor, DODIX. Don't confuse the two roles.

About PIMIX

PIMCO Income under Dan Ivascyn became the post-Gross flagship for a reason: since 2007 it has generated standout income-oriented returns, largely through a savvy long-running position in non-agency mortgages bought cheap after the housing crash. Among multisector bond funds, it has been the category's reference point for over a decade.

About DODIX

Bond markets are where active management has its least-bad record — pricing inefficiencies are real, the Agg index weights toward whoever issues the most debt, and a careful credit shop can add measurable value. DODIX is the standing example: a corporate-tilted, patiently managed core bond fund that has outpaced the Aggregate over most long periods, net of its 0.41% fee.
