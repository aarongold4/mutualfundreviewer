The bottom line

TRBCX dazzled through the 2010s, then fell far harder than VIGAX in 2022 — the honest shape of concentrated active growth. At 0.70% vs 0.05%, you're paying for concentration that helps and hurts in roughly equal measure. The index is the lower-variance, lower-cost default.

About TRBCX

For most of the 2010s, TRBCX was the argument for active growth investing: a concentrated book of franchise compounders that lapped the index year after year. Then 2021–2022 happened — the fund fell dramatically harder than the Russell 1000 Growth and gave back years of accumulated edge in months. The full record now reads as a high-beta growth bet: glorious in expansions, punishing in growth bears.

About VIGAX

VIGAX is what you buy when you want the Magnificent-Seven-and-friends exposure of a growth fund without paying a manager 0.70% to deliver what an index does for 0.05%. It's concentrated where growth indexes are always concentrated — the top ten holdings are a huge share of assets, dominated by the mega-cap tech names — so understand that you're buying a focused bet on the market's most expensive companies continuing to earn their multiples.
