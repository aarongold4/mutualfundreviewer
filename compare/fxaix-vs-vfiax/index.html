The bottom line

FXAIX edges it on paper — 0.015% versus 0.04%, no minimum versus $3,000 — but both are so cheap the difference is lunch money per year. Your brokerage decides this one.

About FXAIX

FXAIX is what happens when index investing becomes a commodity and price wars do their work: the S&P 500, tracked with surgical precision, for 0.015% — a dollar fifty per year on a $10,000 balance — with no minimum investment. There is no catch in the fund itself. Fidelity loses money or close to it here and earns it back when you eventually use their other services.

About VFIAX

This is the direct descendant of the first index fund ever sold to the public — Jack Bogle's 1976 'folly' that now anchors trillions of dollars in retirement money. VFIAX holds the S&P 500 in its market weights and charges 0.04% to do it. Over the decades, that simple recipe has beaten the large majority of professionally managed large-cap funds, which is either embarrassing for the profession or liberating for you, depending on where you sit.
